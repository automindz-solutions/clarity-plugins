## The full ICP, in James's words

He summarised it at the end of the same answer, and it is wider than the desk list — it
includes a vertical no individual desk owns:

> *"ICP is leadership, improvement of recruitment within **healthcare, financial services,
> finance and accounting, technology, built environment, life sciences, and energy**."*

**Energy** appears here and nowhere in the desk mapping. Treat the ICP list as the target
market and the desk table as who currently covers what — they are not the same thing, and
energy work has no named owner.

Use this for routing, not for exclusion: a contact who sits in someone else's vertical is worth
flagging as a hand-off, not dropping. James and Josh both touch technology-adjacent ground, so
never treat the mapping as exclusive.

## Levels

**Candidates:** AVP (Associate Vice President) level through to board.
**Clients / BD targets:** as senior as possible — **CEO, COO, CSO, CRO, Founder, President**.

James on where the line sits: *"A president isn't quite board level, but it's close to it."*
So president is in; a rung below it needs justifying.

A worked example of his own targeting, from the Lemlist run he described on the same call
([9:04](https://fathom.video/share/E2oDhRg9Pn_yjdJ5hTCEMJW833DJpM6r?timestamp=544)): CEO / COO
/ CRO / CSO / President at **US healthcare staffing firms above ~50 headcount**, biased toward
firms *"most likely to hire leaders that are either in locums staffing, or possibly interested
in getting into it."* Note the shape — title band, plus size floor, plus a hiring-intent
hypothesis. A list built on titles alone is not what he asked for.

Clarity operates VP through board. Someone below AVP is out of scope, and in a chase list or
shortlist they are noise rather than a near-miss.

## The title-inflation warning — apply it every time

James's own caution about US titles
([23:18](https://fathom.video/calls/769890926?timestamp=1398)):

> *"It's really hard to understand — all of a sudden you're getting, like, a **Director of
> Delivery, and they manage two people**, you know what I mean? It's like, okay, they're
> not..."*

So a US title alone does not establish seniority. Where a record carries corroborating
evidence — team size managed, billings, reporting line, P&L — weigh that over the title. Where
it does not, say the seniority is unverified rather than inferring it from the words. This is
the same discipline the Fact Find applies: team size and billings are questions on the
qualification checklist precisely because the title does not answer them.
