# Clarity R2R — Candidate ICP / Minimum Placeable Candidate

> Who Clarity places. Use this for shortlisting, sourcing, speccing, scoring, and deciding whether a
> candidate is worth a consultant's time.
> **This is the supply side. For who Clarity sells to, read `icp-clients.md`. Never merge the two lists.**

---

## 0. The shape of a Clarity candidate

A Clarity candidate is **a recruiter who is senior enough that hiring them changes a client's
revenue**, not just their capacity.

They come from recruitment agencies, staffing firms or search firms. They own a desk, a team, a
market, or a P&L. They have a billing track record. The client is buying their book, their market
knowledge and their ability to build under them.

The single most useful mental test: **would an agency owner hire this person to open or fix a
desk?** If yes, they're placeable. If they'd be hired as a pair of hands, they're not.

---

## 1. Seniority: the floor

James, 4 August 2026:

> *"That's from VP level all the way through to board level... from a candidate point of view we
> would always look for people at AVP level, Associate Vice President level, all the way through to
> board level."*

**Floor: Associate Vice President (AVP). Sweet spot: VP through board.**

### Titles in scope

- Associate Vice President (AVP)
- Vice President (VP)
- Senior Vice President (SVP)
- Managing Director (MD)
- Regional Director / National Director
- Head of Practice / Head of [Vertical]
- Practice Lead
- Partner (where genuinely a leadership role with a P&L, not a solo-operator label)
- C-suite of staffing firms: CEO, COO, CRO, CSO, CFO
- Senior Billing Manager equivalents ⚠️ see below

### Out of scope

- Individual contributors, recruiters, consultants, resourcers, sourcers, delivery staff
- Internal / in-house talent acquisition (unless moving into agency leadership, which is rare and
  should be flagged, not auto-included)
- Anyone whose seniority is title-only with no team, no desk and no P&L

⚠️ **CONFIRM with James. This one matters, it changes the shortlist filter:**
The June 2026 spec set the floor at **"Director and above"** and explicitly listed Director,
Associate Director and Billing Manager as in-scope. On 4 August James set the floor at **AVP**.
In US staffing, AVP typically sits above Director, so this is a **tightening**, not a restatement.
Two things to resolve:
1. Are Director and Associate Director now **out**, or still in as a bench tier?
2. Is a **senior Billing Manager** with a large personal book in scope, given the title sits below
   AVP but the commercial value can be higher?

Until James rules, the safe behaviour is: **rank AVP-and-above first, include strong
Director-level and senior-biller profiles in a clearly labelled second tier** rather than dropping
them silently.

## 2. Vertical and market fit

A candidate is only strong if their desk maps to a market Clarity's clients hire into.

| Vertical | Desk owner |
|---|---|
| Healthcare / healthcare staffing / locums | James |
| Technology | Billy, Josh |
| Life Sciences | Billy |
| Built Environment (incl. data centres) | Billy |
| Finance & Accounting, Financial Services | Marcus |
| Energy | ⚠️ Unassigned. Ask. |

**Geography:** United States, all states. Metro matters for fit; a Chicago financial-services leader
is not a swap for a San Francisco one. Always surface location and flag relocation assumptions
rather than treating location as soft.

**Background requirement:** recruitment / staffing / executive search industry. Agency-side, not
in-house.

## 3. Scoring a candidate (0–10, pass at 7+)

Score on evidence, and say what the evidence was.

| Dimension | What earns the score |
|---|---|
| **Seniority match** | Exact title match beats adjacent beats aspirational. Real team or desk ownership beats a title. |
| **Vertical match** | Same vertical as the role beats adjacent (e.g. life sciences ↔ healthcare) beats unrelated. |
| **Firm-type match** | Agency / staffing / search background. An in-house TA leader is a different animal; flag, don't auto-score. |
| **Tenure and stability** | 2+ years in the current role is a strong signal. A three-job-in-three-years pattern is a flag, not a disqualifier: note it. |
| **Location** | Same metro or a credible commute. Explicit remote history counts. |
| **Warmth** | Already in Loxo, already a first-degree connection, already DM'd. This is a multiplier, not a tiebreaker. |
| **Activity signals** | Recent LinkedIn activity, profile updates, "open to work", a recent post about their market. |

**Always state where the candidate came from** (Loxo / Kondo / open market) and **whether they're
already in the job's pipeline or genuinely net-new.**

## 4. What makes a candidate genuinely placeable (beyond the profile)

The profile gets them onto the list. These get them placed, and they mostly live in call notes and
transcripts, not in structured fields. Pull them from Loxo activity and VXT / Co-Recruit transcripts.

- **Billing track record.** Numbers, in the currency and period they were stated. This is the single
  most persuasive line on a front sheet.
- **Motivation to move, in their words.** Why now, why leaving, what's missing. Clarity trains hard
  on selling back to what the candidate said at qualification, so capture it verbatim.
- **What they're looking for.** Comp expectation, structure, autonomy, team size, desk type.
- **Compensation.** Current base, commission structure, and target.
- **Notice period and availability.**
- **Process they're already in.** Which firms they're interviewing with, and at what stage. This is
  both an urgency signal and a BD lead (see §6).
- **Desk portability.** Can they bring clients, or is their billing tied to their current firm's
  brand and infrastructure?

**If a field isn't captured, write "not captured."** Do not infer comp, notice or motivation. Front
sheets go to clients and a wrong number is worse than a blank.

## 5. Sourcing order

Always, in this order:

1. **Loxo** (~24,000 contacts). Mine it before anything else. It is the most under-used asset in the
   business and re-engaging it is a stated priority.
2. **The network** — Kondo: first-degree connections, live DM threads, and Billy's saved lists of
   warm-but-not-yet-CRM people. Cross-reference every shortlist against this: a candidate already
   connected is a voice note, not a cold approach.
3. **Open market** — AI Ark. Count before you pull (credits are metered), filter to agency-side, and
   dedup against Loxo before presenting anything.

Present the three tiers separately and label them. The consultant works tier 1 and 2 first.

## 6. The candidate-to-BD loop (do not skip this)

Every candidate Clarity works is also a BD asset. On or after every candidate call:

1. Pull **every previous employer** from their LinkedIn profile
2. For each of those firms, find the C-suite contacts per `icp-clients.md` §2
3. Run the **existing-client guardrail** (`icp-clients.md` §4) over every one
4. Present the survivors for approval, then push to the BD campaign
5. Separately capture **any firm the candidate mentions they're interviewing with** (an active
   hiring signal, straight from the source)
6. Separately capture **candidate referrals** the person gives

Today this is manual and takes seconds per candidate via the Lemlist Chrome extension. Automating it
was called out by James as *"a really important skill for us to have"*, because the team is drilled
on it daily. Approval is mandatory at step 4: previous employers are frequently existing clients.

## 7. Re-engagement

The 24k Loxo database and the first-degree network both go stale. Two standing plays:

- **Cold candidates:** no contact in 90+ days (or never), not Active / Placed / DNC, ICP titles, US.
  Cap at **20–30 per day**. Verify they're still at the same firm; if they've moved, that's a job
  change and routes to BD as a signal instead.
- **Dormant LinkedIn threads:** anyone who hasn't replied to a DM in 6 or 12 months, cross-checked
  against Loxo. A thread that went quiet after a booked call is the highest-value re-engagement
  there is.

## 8. Speccing (candidate to market)

When Clarity has a strong candidate, they market them out. The judgement is two-layered:

1. **Which client firms fit this candidate** — market, live roles, hiring history, size, growth
2. **Who inside each firm to float them to** — can they hire, does Clarity already have a
   relationship, and can we reach them

Rank on hiring authority first, existing relationship second, reachability third. Produce a review
list, stop there, and let the consultant decide who's actually worth the call. The relationship
politics that decide this are frequently not in the data.
