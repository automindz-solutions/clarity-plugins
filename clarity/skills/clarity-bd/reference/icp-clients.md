# Clarity R2R — Client ICP (business development)

> Who Clarity sells to. Use this for every BD list, every lead qualification, every outreach
> sequence, every signal check.
> **This is the buy-side. For who Clarity places, read `icp-candidates.md`. Never merge the two lists.**

---

## 0. The one thing to get right first

Clarity is recruiter-to-recruiter, so *every* company in the database is a recruitment business.
That means "is this a staffing firm?" is not a useful filter on its own. The real filters are:

1. **Are they the kind of staffing firm that hires senior leadership?**
2. **Is the person we're contacting senior enough to make that decision?**
3. **Are we already engaged with them?**

Get those three right and the list is good. Get any one wrong and the list is noise.

## 1. Target company profile

| Criterion | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Company type** | Recruitment, staffing and executive search agencies |
| **Geography** | United States, all states |
| **Sectors they recruit into** | Healthcare (incl. locums), Financial Services, Finance & Accounting, Technology, Built Environment, Life Sciences, Energy |
| **Growth posture** | Scaling, opening desks, entering new verticals, post-funding, hiring at leadership level. The word James uses is "hypergrowth." |
| **Size** | Mid-size and scaling. Previously specified as 11–500 employees. ⚠️ See below. |
| **Model** | Firms doing substantial-value placements; retained and exclusive work preferred |

### Hard exclusions

- **Enterprise search giants** (Korn Ferry, Heidrick, and similar). They hire through their own
  internal TA and won't use an R2R firm.
- **10,000+ employee staffing firms.** Same reason.
- **High-volume, blue-collar and manual-labour staffing.** Wrong economics, wrong seniority.
- **Non-staffing companies**, unless there's an obvious crossover (e.g. an internal TA leadership
  hire at a firm that also runs an RPO).
- **Internal talent acquisition teams at non-recruitment companies.** Clarity places into agencies.
  When sourcing or list-building in AI Ark, this is the single most common false positive: filter to
  agency-side, not in-house TA.

restated since. Two questions: (a) is the floor still 11, given a 12-person boutique may not hire a
VP; (b) is the ceiling still 500, or should it flex where a larger firm has an autonomous desk.

## 2. Who to contact (decision-maker titles)

James, on the 4 August call, unprompted:

> *"With regards to targeting clients, that will always be as most senior as we can. So that is CEO,
> COO, CSO, CRO, Founder, or President."*

### INCLUDE

- Founder / Co-Founder
- CEO
- President
- COO
- CRO (Chief Revenue Officer)
- CSO (Chief Sales Officer)

### EXCLUDE

- **Any Director-level title.** James is explicit and he has a reason: US staffing title inflation
  means a "Director of Delivery" may manage two people and hire nobody. His words: *"all of a sudden
  you're getting a director of delivery and they manage two people. They're not a good target."*
- **Partner.** Too generic in search firms; often a solo operator with no hiring authority.
- **Managing Director / EVP.** ⚠️ These were on the approved list in the June spec but James did not
  restate them on 4 August. **Decide before the next list ships.** Recommendation: keep MD in for
  US firms where MD is genuinely the top of a P&L, exclude where it is a senior-biller title. Ask
  James.
- Recruiters, consultants, delivery staff, internal TA, HR, operations.

### The rule underneath the rule

The title list is a proxy for one question: **can this person decide to hire a leader and sign
terms?** If a title is ambiguous, check headcount, LinkedIn bio and whether they appear to own a
P&L. When in doubt, flag it for the consultant rather than including it.

**Build an exclude-titles input** so James can add his own exclusions without coming back to us.
This was agreed and is still outstanding.

## 3. BD signals, in priority order

Warm beats cold, always. James and Billy both strongly prefer outreach that references something
real over a generic pitch.

**Tier 1 — people movement**
1. A director-and-above leaves a staffing agency → backfill opportunity, contact the firm they left
2. A director-and-above joins a staffing agency → ramp-up, they'll build a team under them
3. Promotion announcements at target firms ("promoted to VP / MD / Director")

**Tier 2 — stated growth intent (LinkedIn posts, press, site changes)**
- "We're hiring" / "looking for recruiters" / "join our team"
- "Expanding the team" / "opening a new desk" / "new division" / "new office"
- "Record quarter" / "we're scaling" / "fastest-growing"
- "Building out [vertical]" / "growing our [city] team"
- "Overwhelmed with req load" / "need more capacity"

**Tier 3 — inferred**
- Funding, acquisition, or a new-market announcement
- A high volume of open roles posted by the agency itself
- **Candidate-derived:** every previous employer of a candidate Clarity is working, plus any firm a
  candidate mentions they're interviewing with. This is Clarity's best-converting BD source and
  should be treated as a first-class signal, not an afterthought.

**Signal scoring:** rate 1–5 for strength. Only pass **3 and above** into an outreach queue.
Always include the specific evidence (the post, the move, the date) so the consultant can reference
it in the message.

## 4. The existing-client guardrail — read before every list ships

This is the rule most likely to embarrass Clarity if we get it wrong, and it is more subtle than it
looks. From James, 4 August:

> *"We have got terms agreed with a lot of businesses that we haven't necessarily made a placement
> with. So we need to be careful of that."*

**Do not use "have we placed someone there" as the test.** A firm counts as engaged, and is
therefore off-limits for cold BD, if **any** of the following are true in Loxo:

- ✅ A placement exists
- ✅ Terms have been agreed (even with no placement)
- ✅ Interview activity is logged against the company
- ✅ There's a recent email or call thread with anyone there
- ✅ There's an active or recent Lemlist campaign touching that firm
- ✅ Anyone at the firm has been contacted in the last 60 days
- ✅ The firm or contact is flagged DNC

**Also check Kondo:** an active LinkedIn DM thread is contact, even if it never reached Loxo.

**Process:** run the check, then **route the borderline cases to the desk owner rather than deciding
yourself.** Loxo client status is not always kept current (Clarity know this), so the note history is
often more reliable than the status field. When a firm is excluded, say which test it failed, so the
consultant can override if they know better.

**Known live example:** GQR is James's client in healthcare as of August 2026. Nobody from GQR goes
into a healthcare BD campaign.

## 6. Outreach mechanics

- **Lemlist is the sequencing engine and the port of entry for replies.** Replies route back to the
  consultant and get logged to Loxo.
- **Never enrol anyone without approval.** Present the list, show the exclusion checks that ran,
  wait for the yes.
- **Personalise on the signal, not on the person.** Reference the post, the hire, the expansion.
  Generic personalisation ("love what you're building") is worse than none.
- **Watch capacity.** Lemlist has over-delivered replies before and James has had to switch
  campaigns off. Pace re-engagement at **20–30 per day** unless told otherwise.
- Warm and high-value targets can carry a SendSpark video or a Lemlist voice note step.
